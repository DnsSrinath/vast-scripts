# Indian Law for the Common Man

**A plain-language research project: how many laws India has, what the main law books say, and what every citizen should know.**

Research date: 5 September 2026. This is analysis and explanation only, not legal advice. Laws change; always check the current text on [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in) or ask a lawyer or your District Legal Services Authority (free helpline 15100).

## The short answer: how many laws does India have?

| What | Number (Sept 2026) | How sure |
|---|---|---|
| Constitution of India | 1 (about 470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules, 106 amendments) | Verified |
| Central Acts in force (made by Parliament) | 859 (845 live + 14 "spent"), not counting yearly Finance Acts | Verified (Ministry of Law & Justice / India Code) |
| State Acts in force (made by 28 states + UTs) | Several thousand; no single official total | Estimate |
| Rules, regulations, notifications under those Acts | Tens of thousands | Estimate |
| Central Acts repealed as obsolete since 2014 | About 1,500 | Verified |

So the honest answer is: **one Constitution, roughly 860 central laws, a few thousand state laws, and a very large body of rules under them.** Nobody, including the government, publishes one exact grand total. The chapters below explain why, and which small set of laws actually matters in daily life.

## A note on the question "Indian EPCO rules"

The request used the word "EPCO". There is no Indian law called EPCO. The closest real meanings are the old **IPC** (Indian Penal Code, now replaced by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023), the **EPFO** (Employees' Provident Fund Organisation and its rules), and Madhya Pradesh's **EPCO** (Environmental Planning and Coordination Organisation). Chapter 6 covers all three, and the whole project answers the broader question of how many laws India has and how to make them understandable.

## How this research was done

One architect model planned the work and cross-checked headline numbers. Six research agents (Opus and Sonnet models) did web research in parallel on counts, law books, the Constitution, the legal machinery, everyday situations, and reforms. Every chapter lists its sources. Anything that could not be verified from a reliable source is marked as an estimate or as unverified.
