# How Many Laws Does India Have? — A Sourced Count

**Research date:** 5 September 2026
**Question:** "How many laws does India have in total?"

---

## 0. Method and an important caveat about this research

This environment's network egress proxy blocked all direct page fetching (including
`indiacode.nic.in`, `legislative.gov.in`, `prsindia.org`, `en.wikipedia.org`, and even
`example.com`; `curl` to the Legislative Department PDF returned `CONNECT tunnel failed,
response 403`). **All findings below were obtained through a web *search* tool that returns
result links plus extracted snippets, not through direct retrieval of the primary documents.**

Consequently every figure is tagged:

- **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]** — the number comes from an official source (India Code / Legislative
  Department / PIB / Parliament) *as quoted in the retrieved snippet*, and the primary URL is given.
- **[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]** — a reputable non-official source (PRS, ORF, Vidhi, SCC Online,
  Wikipedia legal-list pages, mainstream legal press) states the figure.
- **[ESTIMATE]** — a derived, ranged, or order-of-magnitude figure. No authority publishes it.

Anyone relying on this for legal or policy work should re-open the India Code portal and the
Legislative Department PDFs directly to re-confirm the counts marked PRIMARY.

**The short answer:** There is no single number, and there cannot be. "Laws" is not one category.
India has roughly **850 central Acts in force**, plausibly **several thousand to a few tens of
thousands of state Acts**, and **tens of thousands of live rules/regulations** sitting on top of
them, plus hundreds of thousands of notifications, orders and circulars. Anyone who quotes a
single tidy total is either counting only central Acts or making it up.

## 2. Central Acts in force

### 2.1 The two figures currently in circulation

**(a) 836 Central Acts** — On **13 August 2026** the Ministry of Law and Justice relaunched the
India Code portal at `indiacode.gov.in` (AI-assisted semantic and speech search, Eighth Schedule
multilingual access, e-Gazette links, QR-enabled documents), launched by Law Minister Arjun Ram
Meghwal. A corpus audit dated **28 August 2026** reports the live site holding **836 Central Acts,
1,410 State Acts and 77,072 sections**.
Source: eCourtsIndia blog, "IndiaCode vs India Code (indiacode.gov.in)", 30 Aug 2026 —
<https://blogs.ecourtsindia.com/2026/08/30/indiacode-vs-indiacode-gov-in/>
**[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**

**(b) 859 Central Acts** — The maintained list of Acts of Parliament, whose compilers state it is
drawn from the Ministry of Law and Justice website and India Code and is current to **July 2026**,
gives **859 Central Acts still in force apart from Finance Acts — 845 Acts plus 14 spent Acts**.
Source: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_acts_of_the_Parliament_of_India>
**[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**

### 2.2 Why the two disagree, and what to quote

The gap (~23) is a definitional and data-hygiene gap, not a dispute about the law:

- Whether **Finance Acts and Appropriation Acts** are counted. There is one Finance Act a year and
  several Appropriation Acts; including the whole historical run adds hundreds of technically
  unrepealed but wholly spent statutes. Both figures above exclude most of them; the "14 spent
  Acts" line in (b) shows the compilers separating them out explicitly.
- Whether **spent, obsolete-but-unrepealed, and regionally-limited pre-1947 Acts** are counted.
- Whether Acts already repealed by the **Repealing and Amending Act, 2025** (assented Dec 2025 /
  gazetted early 2026) have been purged from the portal yet. The 836 figure is post-2025-repeal;
  the 859 figure may lag.
- Portal ingestion lag on very recent Acts.

**Recommended phrasing:** *"Roughly 830–890 central Acts are in force; the India Code corpus as of
late August 2026 holds 836."* The commonly repeated journalistic figure of **"about 880–890 central
Acts"** was accurate a few years ago and is now slightly high after the 2023 and 2025 repeal rounds.
**[ESTIMATE]**

### 2.3 How many central Acts have *ever* existed

**6,779 Central Acts** had been enacted since **1834** as at the Finance Act, 2019 (Act 7 of 2019).
So roughly **87% of all central Acts India has ever passed have been repealed, spent or superseded.**
Source: SCC Times, "Central Acts of India (1834–2019) Repealed and Unrepealed", 31 Jan 2020 —
<https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2020/01/31/central-acts-of-india-1834-2019-repealed-and-unrepealed-know-and-understand-the-history-of-law-making-of-india/>
**[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**

PRS Legislative Research separately records **over 3,800 Acts passed since the first post-independence
general elections in 1952**, with legislative output falling from an average of **72 Bills/year in the
1st Lok Sabha to ~40 Bills/year by the 15th**; the single busiest year was **1976 (118 Bills)** and the
quietest **2004 (18 Bills)**.
Source: <https://prsindia.org/articles-by-prs-team/indian-parliament-at-60-years-facts-statistics>
**[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**

Legislative Department, identified **1,824 redundant and obsolete Central Acts** for repeal. At the
2015 ET Global Business Summit the Prime Minister put the figure at **1,877 laws identified for repeal**.
**[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**

## 3. State Acts — why nobody has an exact total

### 3.1 What India Code shows

India Code is described officially as "a digital repository of **all** Central, State & Union
Territory enactments/legislations, and their subordinate legislations that are in force with
up-to-date content."
Source: <https://services.india.gov.in/service/detail/india-code-digital-repository-of-all-central-and-state-acts>
**[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**

In practice it is nowhere near complete for states. Two data points that cannot both be right:

- The **corpus audit of 28 Aug 2026 counts 1,410 State Acts** across the entire live portal.
  **[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**
- Yet the **per-state browse pages** on India Code show, individually:
  **Assam 1,129 · West Bengal 804 · Kerala 523 · Chhattisgarh 510 · Rajasthan 487** —
  five states alone totalling **3,453**, more than double the portal-wide "1,410".
  Source: India Code state landing pages, e.g. <https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2513/>
  (Assam), <https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2180> (State Act index).
  **[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**

The most likely reconciliation is that "1,410" counts only Acts fully ingested into the new
sectioned corpus of the relaunched portal, while the per-state pages count all listed enactments
including PDF-only entries. **I could not resolve this without fetching the portal, which was
blocked.** Flag it as an open item.

### 3.2 Why there is no authoritative national total

1. **No central register exists.** Each state legislature and Law Department maintains its own
   statute book. There is no constitutional or statutory obligation on states to report a count to
   the Union, and the Legislative Department's remit is central law.
2. **States repeal badly.** Repealed state Acts frequently remain listed. The Centre for Civil
   Society's Repeal of Laws project — which analysed **over 530 outdated laws and secured repeal of
   227**, publishing state compendiums for Assam, Gujarat, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Tripura, J&K,
   Chhattisgarh (25 statutes), Telangana (26 statutes) and others — titled one of its own reviews
   *"The Old Order Changeth Not: the states have been lethargic in getting rid of old statutes."*
   Sources: <https://ccs.in/repeal-laws> · <https://ccs.in/repeal-laws-tracker> ·
   <https://old.ccs.in/old-order-changeth-not-states-have-been-lethargic-getting-rid-old-statutes-need-be-repealed>
   **[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**
3. **Territorial reorganisation multiplies entries.** When a state splits (UP/Uttarakhand, MP/
   Chhattisgarh, Bihar/Jharkhand, AP/Telangana, J&K/Ladakh), the parent state's Acts are inherited
   by the successor and typically counted **twice**. Assam's outsize 1,129 partly reflects inherited
   Acts across the whole reorganised North-East.
4. **Pre-Constitution provincial and princely-state law survives** under Article 372, much of it
   never catalogued.
5. **Definitional drift.** Do you count Regulations for UTs? Ordinances later lapsed? Acts in force
   only in part of a state?

### 3.3 Best available range

| Basis | Figure | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| India Code new-corpus count | 1,410 | VERIFIED-SECONDARY (clearly incomplete) |
| Sum of India Code per-state listings (extrapolating the five known states, avg ~690, across 28 states + 8 UTs) | **~15,000–25,000** | ESTIMATE (my extrapolation) |
| Commonly quoted upper figure including all state enactments ever placed on state statute books | **30,000+** | ESTIMATE — widely repeated, **not traceable to any authority I could verify** |
| Conservative "substantively live and used" state Acts | **~3,000–5,000** | ESTIMATE |

**Recommended phrasing:** *"Nobody knows. India Code's state coverage is incomplete; per-state
listings imply the order of 15,000–25,000 state enactments on the books, of which only a few
thousand are substantively live. Figures of '30,000+' circulate but have no traceable authority."*

## 4. Subordinate legislation: rules, regulations, notifications, orders, bye-laws

This is where the true volume of Indian law sits, and it is the least well counted layer.

- India Code's stated design is that each Act is "linked with Subordinate Data like **Rules,
  Regulations, Notifications, Orders, Circulars, Ordinances, and Statutes**." The portal publishes no
  aggregate count of these.
  Source: <https://services.india.gov.in/service/detail/india-code-digital-repository-of-all-central-and-state-acts>
  **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY for the description; no count available]**
- The most-cited academic figure is that **~60,000 statutory instruments were made in India over
  roughly six decades**. This appears in teaching material on subordinate legislation
  (<https://www.lawweb.in/2025/05/llm-notes-subordinate-legislation-in.html>) and in Association of
  Secretaries General of Parliaments material (<https://asgp.co/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Sheriff.doc>).
  **[ESTIMATE — repeated widely, original derivation not verifiable here. Treat with caution.]**
- The best *operational* proxy is TeamLease RegTech's compliance database, which maps
  **1,536 Acts and Rules**, **69,233 unique compliances** and **6,618 filings** across
  **28 States and 8 Union Territories**, built by crawling **2,000–3,700 government websites**.
  It logged **12,973 compliance updates in 2025** and describes "**over 8,000 changes every year**."
  Sources: <https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/aboutus/> ·
  <https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/regtrack/compliance-management-system/> ·
  <https://group.teamlease.com/services/compliance-management/>
  **[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**

**Order-of-magnitude conclusion [ESTIMATE]:**

| Layer | Order of magnitude |
|---|---|
| Central Acts | 10^3 (~850) |
| State Acts | 10^4 (~15,000–25,000) |
| Rules & Regulations (central + state), live | 10^4 (tens of thousands) |
| Notifications, orders, circulars, bye-laws | 10^5–10^6 (hundreds of thousands to millions; ~8,000–13,000 *changes* alone per year) |

A useful line for a lay reader: **for every Act of Parliament there are on the order of ten to a
hundred subordinate instruments made under it, and those instruments — not the Acts — are what
actually bind a citizen or a business.**

## 5. The Constitution of India

### 5.1 Articles, Parts, Schedules

| | At commencement (26 Jan 1950) | Today (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Articles | **395** | **448** (see counting caveat) |
| Parts | **22** | **25** |
| Schedules | **8** | **12** |

Amendments added four new Parts (one of which — Part VII, on Part B States — was later repealed by
the 7th Amendment, 1956) and four new Schedules (9th, 1951; 10th, 1985; 11th and 12th, 1992–93).
Sources: <https://www.constitutionofindia.net/read/> · <https://www.clearias.com/constitution-of-india/> ·
<https://vajiramandravi.com/upsc-exam/schedules-of-the-indian-constitution/>
**[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**

**The counting caveat — this is why you see 395, 448 and ~470 all quoted:**

- **395** is the *highest article number*, and it is unchanged since 1950. The numbering scheme was
  frozen: new provisions were inserted as lettered articles (21A, 31A–31D, 51A, 243A–243ZG,
  300A, 323A–323B, 338A/338B, 371A–371J, 394A) and deleted ones (31, 238, 305, 320(4), Part VII)
  were left as gaps.
- **448** is the standard current count of *operative* articles, i.e. 395 minus repealed articles
  plus lettered insertions. This is the figure to use.
- **"~470"** is what you get if you count every lettered sub-article separately, including the full
  243A–243ZG panchayat/municipality run and the 371 series. It is not wrong, just a different
  counting convention. **The premise "now ~470 articles" in the research brief is a variant
  counting convention, not the standard figure; 448 is the standard.** **[ESTIMATE / convention note]**

As of **March 2026** the maintained amendment list still records **106 amendments since 1950**, and
nothing found in this research indicates any amendment enacted between then and 5 September 2026.

<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Hundred_and_Sixth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_India>
**[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**

## 6. Law Commission of India, colonial-era survivals, and the oldest laws

### 6.1 Law Commission reports

- **289 reports** have been submitted since 1955. The highest-numbered report identified is
  **Report No. 289, "Trade Secrets and Economic Espionage."** Recent reports include No. 287 (matrimonial
  issues of NRIs/OCIs) and No. 286 (review of the Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897).
  *Caveat:* the Law Commission's own website index still shows "277 reports" in places, i.e. the
  official landing page lags its own publications. Some secondary sources say "more than 280."
  Sources: <https://lawcommissionofindia.nic.in/law-commission-reports/> ·
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Commission_of_India>
  **[VERIFIED-SECONDARY, with an acknowledged discrepancy]**
- The **23rd Law Commission** was constituted for a three-year term, **1 September 2024 to 31 August 2027**.
  Sources: <https://lawcommissionofindia.nic.in/document/the-23rd-law-commission/> ·
  <https://www.legalaffairs.gov.in/sectiondivision/23rd-law-commission-india>
  **[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**

### 6.2 What the Law Commission said about the *volume* of laws

The 20th Law Commission ran a dedicated study titled **"Legal Enactments: Simplification and
Streamlining" (LESS)** and issued four interim reports on Obsolete Laws:

| Report | Date | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **No. 248** (First Interim) | 12 Sep 2014 | **72** laws obsolete, recommended for immediate repeal |
| **No. 249** (Second Interim) | 13 Oct 2014 | **88** for wholesale repeal, **25** for partial repeal |
| **No. 250** (Third Interim) | 2014–15 | **73** more for complete repeal |
| **No. 251** (Fourth Interim) | 2015 | **54** studied, **30** recommended for complete repeal |

The Commission's own impact page records that these four reports "resulted in repeal by the
legislature of over **1,500 central Acts**." Report No. 249 also examined **253 laws recommended for
repeal by the earlier PC Jain Commission Report**.
Sources: <https://lawcommissionofindia.nic.in/achievements-impact-of-law-commission-report/> ·
<https://indiankanoon.org/doc/129259806/> (No. 248) · <https://indiankanoon.org/doc/151043469/> (No. 249) ·
<https://indiankanoon.org/doc/167477050/> (No. 250) · <https://indiankanoon.org/doc/38894497/> (No. 251) ·
<https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ca0daec69b5adc880fb464895726dbdf/uploads/2022/08/2022081638.pdf> (No. 249 PDF)
**[VERIFIED-PRIMARY]**

The Commission's substantive argument — consistent across all four reports — is that statutory
**volume is itself a rule-of-law problem**: a citizen cannot know the law when the statute book
contains hundreds of enactments that are spent, superseded, territorially defunct, or simply
forgotten, and when repeal is the only mechanism and it is used sparingly.

### 6.3 The oldest Acts still in force

Two Acts of **1836** are the oldest enactments still on the Indian statute book:

- **Bengal Districts Act, 1836 (Act 21 of 1836)** — empowers the State Government to create new
  districts by notification in the Official Gazette. Other states now do this under their Revenue
  Codes, but **West Bengal still does it under this 1836 central Act**. Recommended for repeal by
  the Law Commission *and* by the PC Jain Commission; **not repealed; still operative.**
  Sources: <https://www.commonlii.org/in/legis/cen/num_act/bda1836168/> ·
  <https://indiatogether.org/law-commission-report-seeking-repeal-of-obsolete-laws-laws>
- **Bengal Indigo Contracts Act, 1836 (Act 10 of 1836)** — enacted by the Governor-General-in-Council
  on **11 April 1836**; concerned disputes over indigo cultivation contracts. Recommended for
  immediate repeal in Law Commission Report No. 249. **Its India Code entry and bare text were still
  live in 2026** (`handle/123456789/18935`, `a1836-10.pdf`).
  Sources: <https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/18935?view_type=browse> ·
  <https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/18935/1/a1836-10.pdf> ·
  <https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/lawreports/obsoletelaws2/2.php?STitle=Bengal+Indigo+Contracts+Act%2C+Act+10+of+1836>

spanning 1886–2023, and its stated earliest year is 1886, which *suggests* the 1836 Act was NOT
included and therefore survives. Verify against the Act's Schedule at
<https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22046/1/a2025-37.pdf>.]**

### 6.4 Pre-independence Acts still in force

**No authoritative count exists** of how many pre-1947 Acts remain in force — I searched for one
specifically and none is published. What is established:

- **Article 372** continued all pre-Constitution laws in force until altered or repealed, so nothing
  lapsed automatically in 1947 or 1950.
- Substantial nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century statutes remain the operative law of everyday
  life: the **Indian Contract Act, 1872**, the **Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881** (cheque
  dishonour), the **Transfer of Property Act, 1882**, the **Indian Evidence Act, 1872** (until 2024),
  the **Code of Civil Procedure, 1908**, the **Indian Stamp Act, 1899**, the **Registration Act,
  1908**, the **Land Acquisition** framework, and the **Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897**.
- The three great colonial criminal codes were finally replaced with effect from **1 July 2024** by
  the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (IPC 1860), Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (CrPC 1973/1898) and
  Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (Evidence Act 1872).
Sources: <https://blog.ipleaders.in/colonial-laws-that-are-still-prevalent-in-modern-india/> ·
<https://thefederal.com/the-federal-special/what-makes-a-law-colonial-india-oldest-statutes-still-live-on-253635> ·
<https://prsindia.org/billtrack/overview-of-criminal-law-reforms>
**[VERIFIED-SECONDARY for the examples; the *count* is NOT AVAILABLE]**

**Rough estimate:** given 836 central Acts in force and that India Code's chronological central Acts
list runs from 1836 to date, **on the order of 200–350 of the central Acts in force predate 1947.**

## 7. How many laws does a typical citizen actually interact with?

**There is no study that answers this directly.** I searched specifically for one from Vidhi,
ORF, PRS and academic sources and found none. What exists are three useful proxies.

### 7.1 The criminalisation surface (Vidhi) — the best available proxy

Vidhi Centre for Legal Policy's **"The State of the System" (S.O.S.)**, launched April 2022 and
hosted at **crimeandpunishment.in**, is the first complete database of criminal offences in Indian
central law. Findings:

- **7,305 distinct crimes** defined across **370 central Acts**.
- That is roughly **42% of all central laws carrying criminal penalties.**
- Coverage spans **174 years of legislative enactment across 45 subject matters.**
- **Over 75% of these offences have nothing to do with public safety** — they sit "in the rules of
  daily governance, regulating shipping, taxes, and town councils."

Sources: <https://vidhilegalpolicy.in/research/the-state-of-the-system/> · <https://crimeandpunishment.in/sos> ·
The Wire, "A Licence to Punish: How 7,300 'Crimes' Make Criminals of Us All" —
<https://m.thewire.in/article/law/a-licence-to-punish-how-7300-crimes-make-criminals-of-us-all>
**[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**

This is the sharpest available answer to the citizen question: **a citizen is exposed to roughly
7,300 criminal offences under central law alone**, before any state law.

### 7.2 The business proxy

An **average Indian MSME in manufacturing with more than 150 employees deals with 500–900
compliances a year** (ORF/TeamLease). **[VERIFIED-SECONDARY]**

### 7.3 The practical answer

For an ordinary individual not running a business, the set of statutes actually touched in daily
life is **small — on the order of 15 to 40 enactments**: the Constitution; BNS/BNSS/BSA; the Motor
Vehicles Act, 1988; the Consumer Protection Act, 2019; the Right to Information Act, 2005; the
Income-tax Act (1961, replaced by the Income-tax Act, 2025) and GST laws; the Contract Act, 1872;
the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881; the Transfer of Property and Registration Acts; personal law
statutes (Hindu Marriage Act 1955, Special Marriage Act 1954, etc.); the Right to Education Act,
2009; labour codes; the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its 2025 Rules; state Shops
and Establishments, tenancy, excise, stamp duty and municipal legislation.

**[ESTIMATE — this list is my synthesis. No study quantifies it. Popular "laws every Indian should
know" compilations converge on 10–19 items, which is a floor, not a measurement:
<https://indialegallive.com/legal/18-laws-and-rights-every-indian-should-know/>]**

**The honest framing:** the *number of laws a citizen touches* is small; the *number of legal
obligations embedded in them* is enormous. That gap is the whole subject of the next section.

## 9. Bottom line

**"How many laws does India have?" has no single correct answer. The defensible answer is layered:**

1. **Central Acts in force: ~836–859** (call it **~850**), down from roughly **1,200–1,400 in 2014**
   after the repeal of **1,500+** obsolete central Acts between 2014 and 2026. India has enacted
   **6,779** central Acts in total since 1834, so ~87% are gone.
2. **State Acts: unknown and unknowable to a precise figure.** India Code's per-state listings imply
   **~15,000–25,000**; only a few thousand are substantively live. The "30,000+" figure that
   circulates has no traceable authority.
3. **Subordinate legislation dwarfs both:** tens of thousands of live rules and regulations,
   hundreds of thousands of notifications and orders, changing at **8,000–13,000 amendments a year.**
4. **The Constitution:** **448 articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules**, amended **106 times**, the last
   in September 2023. Three amendment Bills numbered 129–131 have been introduced since; **one was
   defeated (131st, 17 April 2026)** and two remain with Joint Parliamentary Committees.
5. **What actually binds people:** **7,305 criminal offences across 370 central Acts**;
   **69,233 compliances**, of which **26,134 carry a prison sentence**, for businesses.
   Two Jan Vishwas Acts (2023, 2026) have decriminalised about **900 provisions** across ~121 Acts.

**The single most useful sentence for a lay audience:** *India has around 850 central Acts and
perhaps twenty thousand state Acts, but the laws that actually govern daily life are the tens of
thousands of rules and the 69,233 compliance obligations built on top of them — and until recently
about two in five of those could put you in jail.*
